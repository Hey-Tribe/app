Chores

How to split chores fairly in a family

October 18, 20264 min read

Fair does not have to mean equal. It means everyone can see who does what, and nobody is quietly doing most of it.

Almost every household argues about chores at some point. Most of the time the argument is about how the work is divided, and less about the work itself. Somebody feels like they do more, somebody else feels like they never get credit, and nobody has a clear picture of who does what.

Splitting chores fairly starts with getting that picture on paper. Here is a way to do it that works for couples, for parents with kids at home and for houses with a few adults sharing space.

Fair does not mean identical

A fair split is one that everyone involved agrees is fair. That can look different in every house. If one adult works long shifts outside the home, the other may take more of the daytime jobs. If a teenager has a big week of exams, someone else covers the dishes. What matters is that people agree to the arrangement and can see it.

Count the hours when you can. Ask each adult to jot down the household jobs they did for one week, with a rough time next to each. Then compare. People are often surprised in both directions.

Step 1: List every job, including the ones nobody sees

Start with the obvious ones: dishes, laundry, cooking, trash, groceries, cleaning bathrooms. Then add the planning jobs that rarely get counted. Remembering the dentist appointment, noticing the milk is low, buying the birthday gift, signing the school form. If you want to dig into that last group, we wrote about it in sharing the mental load.

Write each job on its own line. A list of thirty or forty items is normal.

Step 2: Pick a way to divide them

There is no single best method. These are the common ones, and many families mix two or three.

MethodWorks well whenWatch for
One owner per jobJobs repeat all the time, like bills, pets or school formsThe owner can end up with a heavy load if the jobs vary a lot in size
Days of the weekEveryone has a steady scheduleOne bad day can derail a whole job, so agree on how to swap
RotationJobs nobody likes, such as bathrooms or trashPeople forget whose turn it is unless the rotation is written down
Pick what you like leastAdults who each have clear dislikesSomeone may dislike everything, so set a minimum for each person
TradeTwo people with different strengthsTrades should even out over a month

Step 3: Give whole jobs, not pieces

A job is only handed off when the other person also handles the remembering. If you own the trash, you know it goes out Tuesday night, you notice when the bin is full and you do not need a reminder. If someone else has to nudge you each week, the job is still partly theirs.

Chores for kids, by age

Kids can do more than many parents expect. Common starting points are putting toys in a bin at ages two and three, setting the table and feeding a pet around four or five, and emptying the dishwasher or taking out recycling from about nine. Every child is different, so use these as a rough guide and adjust.

Our chore list generator builds a starter list for each kid from their age, and you can print it or copy it. The post on chore charts kids will use covers rewards and what to do when the novelty wears off.

When it still feels uneven

  • “I do more than you.” Put the lists side by side. If the numbers back up the feeling, move a job. If they do not, you now both know why it feels that way.
  • Different standards. Agree on what “done” means for each job in a sentence or two. “Dishes done” might mean the sink is empty and the counter is wiped.
  • A job keeps getting missed. Check whether it has a clear owner and a day. Most missed jobs have neither.
  • A kid refuses. Shrink the job, shorten the list or change the reward before you add pressure. Two chores done most days beat six chores done never.

Review it once a month

Schedules change, kids grow and one person's job may suddenly get heavier. Ten minutes on the first of the month is enough to ask what is working and what to swap. Put it on the calendar so it does not slide.
