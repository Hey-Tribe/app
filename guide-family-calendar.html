How to set up a family calendar everyone uses

October 18, 20263 min read

One calendar, a few shared habits and a named driver for every ride will prevent most scheduling surprises.

In a lot of homes the family schedule is spread across a work calendar, a school email, a note on the fridge and one person's memory. Things still get through most weeks, and then a Tuesday arrives with two pickups at the same time and nobody can say how that happened.

The fix is dull and it works. Pick one calendar, agree on a few habits and use it for everything that has a time attached.

Pick one home for family plans

It can be an app, a paper wall calendar or a shared Google calendar. Choose the one the whole household will actually open. If you have a partner who lives in their work calendar, make sure the family one can be seen from it (more on that below). Two calendars that disagree are worse than one imperfect calendar.

Decide what goes on it

A short list keeps it readable:

  • Appointments for anyone in the house: doctor, dentist, haircuts, repairs
  • School days off, early dismissals, concerts and parent nights
  • Practices, games and lessons, with the address
  • Birthdays, parties and visits
  • Trips, including travel days
  • Anything that needs a grown-up to be somewhere at a certain time

Leave out tasks like “buy tape.” Those belong on a list.

Give each person a lane

Seeing one color or row per person makes clashes obvious. If Maria has a meeting at 4 and Alex has a pickup at 4, the overlap shows up before the day does. When a plan involves the whole family, put it on everyone's lane or mark it as a family event.

Name the driver

Many missed pickups come from a plan that had a time and a place but no driver. Add a line for who is taking the kid, even if it is the same person every week. When the answer changes, change it on the calendar and not in a text.

One rule for adding things

The simplest rule is that whoever hears about a plan puts it on the calendar that same day. Do not wait for the other parent to ask. If a form comes home in a backpack, take a photo of the date, add it, and move on.

Handle repeating events once

Practice every Thursday, piano on Mondays, trash day, a standing call with grandma. Set these up one time as repeating events so you are not typing them again each week. In HeyTribe, repeating weekly events are part of Premium. On the free plan you can add each one as it comes.

Let the calendar reach your phone

A calendar nobody looks at does no good. Turn on reminders for plans that matter, and subscribe to the family calendar from the calendar app you already use. HeyTribe has a calendar link you can subscribe to from Google, Apple or Outlook, and phone reminders are free.

Look at the week together

Five minutes before the week starts catches most problems. Read each day out loud, fix the clashes, and decide who drives. The 15-minute Sunday reset shows one way to run that.

Mistakes that make families quit

  • Adding too little, so the calendar looks empty and people stop checking it
  • Adding everything, including tasks, until it is too crowded to read
  • Keeping one extra calendar somewhere, usually at work
  • Only one person ever adds events, which turns the calendar into that person's to-do list
