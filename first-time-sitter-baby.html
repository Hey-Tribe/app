Leaving Your Baby With a Sitter for the First Time

October 18, 20264 min read

The first time away from your baby is hard for most parents. A written handoff, a short first outing and a clear way to check in make it easier for everyone.

Handing your baby to someone else, even for an hour, can feel strange. Many parents worry more than they expected to, and that is normal. A little preparation turns most of the worry into a checklist.

This guide assumes you already have a sitter in mind. If you do not, the guide to hiring a babysitter covers finding and checking one.

Start with a short trial

Do not make the first outing a four-hour dinner. Begin with an hour, perhaps a walk around the block or a coffee nearby. Choose a time when the baby is usually calm and fed, such as after a nap. Every baby is different, so watch your own child’s pattern.

Have the sitter come to your home for the first outing. Familiar surroundings are easier for the baby, and everything is within reach.

Meet before the day

Ask the sitter to come 30 minutes early on the first day, or visit once before. Let the baby see you and the sitter together. Walk through the house and show the sitter where things are. Hand over the baby with you in the room for the first few minutes, then step back.

Write a handoff sheet

One page on the counter beats a stream of texts. Include the following.

  • Your phone numbers, and a second contact nearby
  • The address, in case the sitter needs to call for help
  • The baby’s schedule for the time you are away: feed, nap, diaper
  • Feeding instructions, such as how much milk to warm and where it is stored
  • Allergies and anything the baby must not have, as you have confirmed with your doctor
  • Comfort habits, like a pacifier, a white noise machine or a particular song
  • Where the diapers, wipes, clothes and first aid supplies are
  • Your doctor’s number and the emergency number for your area

Leave medicine instructions only if your doctor has given them, and write them exactly as the doctor gave them. Do not guess.

Agree on how to check in

Decide in advance how often you will hear from the sitter. For a first outing, one text halfway through is plenty. Some parents ask for a photo. Others prefer silence unless something comes up. Pick what lets you relax, and tell the sitter that calls are welcome at any time.

Resist the urge to call every ten minutes. If you notice yourself checking the phone, put it in your bag.

Check your home before the sitter arrives. Put away anything unsafe, clear the kitchen counters, and make sure the baby’s sleeping space is set up the way your doctor advised. Tell the sitter plainly where the baby may sleep and where the baby may not. A calm, tidy house sets the tone.

Say goodbye simply

A short, warm goodbye works better than a long, anxious one. Tell the baby you are leaving and that you will be back, hand over, and go. Babies pick up on mood, and a brisk exit often means fewer tears than a long one. If the baby cries, that is common, and most settle within a few minutes once the parent is out of sight.

When you return

Ask the sitter how it went and what the baby did. Write down feeds, naps and diapers while they are fresh. Thank them and pay as agreed. Then talk about whether to lengthen the next outing.

After the first outing goes well, extend the time a little each round. A second outing of two hours, then an evening of three or four. Each good experience builds your confidence and the sitter’s. If something goes wrong, adjust the sheet and try again instead of giving up.

Plan something small for yourselves during that first hour. A short outing you enjoy makes the next one easier to say yes to.
